## Test Cases

### Annual Taxes

Once a year (in February — period 12, the last period of the calendar year) the Cartel sends the player a tax bill. The bill has two parts: a **land tax** based on how many acres of farmland you own, and an **equipment tax** based on the total resale value of your vehicles + implements. There's also a separate **franchise fee** charged at the same time, computed from the year's revenue using a progressive bracket ladder (similar to income-tax brackets in real life). Owning Cartel shares reduces the franchise fee — every share knocks 1% off, capping at 100% off when you own all 100 shares. All three deductions hit the farm's cash balance on the same period-12 tick, alongside any unrelated end-of-year events (wallet payout, share dividends, crop spoilage). Two prestige perks soften the tax: Land Lawyer cuts the land portion (5% per level, capping at 50% off at L10), and Tax Shelter cuts the equipment portion (same scale).

### Vehicle Financing

The base game lets you click "Buy" in the shop and own any vehicle outright as long as you have the cash. FarmPunk replaces that experience with a financing system: every new vehicle purchase becomes a 3-year loan. You pay 20% down at the moment of purchase (the "down payment"), and the remaining 80% becomes a loan you pay off in 36 monthly installments (one payment fires every period change). Used vehicles, workshop modifications (config changes on already-owned gear), and consumables (seeds, fertilizer, lime) all bypass the financing system and use the vanilla flow. Leasing is disabled entirely. The buy flow checks three things before letting a purchase go through: (1) you have enough cash to cover the down payment, (2) post-down-payment cash stays above the cash reserve threshold (covered by RES-NN), and (3) the new loan plus any existing loans stays under the debt cap (covered by FDA-NN). The downPayment skill reduces the 20% down-payment percentage by 1.2pp per level (so at L10 the down is only 8%).

### Placeable Financing

Placeables are buildings: silos, sheds, barns, fences, sheds, etc. Anything you place via the in-game Construction menu. FarmPunk applies the same 20%-down / 80%-loan financing to placeables priced over $1,000 — that threshold is intentional, so you don't get financing dialogs for $50 fence segments. The total cost includes both the placeable's sticker price AND the engine's terrain-displacement cost (when you place a building on uneven ground, the engine charges extra to flatten the terrain) — both are wrapped into the down-payment + loan math. Below the $1,000 threshold, the vanilla placement flow runs (you pay cash, no loan). Reserve and debt-cap checks apply same as vehicles.

### Land Financing

The surcharge scales with how much land you already own — a small farmer pays 2%, a big farmer pays up to 22% (see SUR-01..03). The vanilla map menu's Buy button is intercepted, same shape as the vehicle shop's Buy interception. There's also a console command `farmPunkBuyLand <farmlandId>` that takes the parcel by numeric ID (visible if you click the parcel in the map menu — the ID is in the parcel info panel).

### Minimum Cash Reserve

### Credit Rating

The credit rating is a 6-letter score (F is worst, A is best) that adjusts the interest rate FarmPunk charges on new loans. Each tier above F is worth a 1% discount on new loans only — existing loans keep whatever rate they were issued at. The system is asymmetric on purpose: defaulting (letting your farm balance go below zero) drops your rating *immediately*, but earning a rating upgrade requires surviving a full year without defaulting and only happens at the period-1 (March) tick. Once you default, the rest of that year is a write-off — recovering positive cash later doesn't undo the strike. This section covers the rules around when the rating moves, when it doesn't, how the rate composition works, and how all of this survives save/load.

### Shares

### Crop Licensing

Each harvestable crop has its own annual "license" with a yearly sales quota. Sell more than the quota of that crop in one year, and the station price for that crop crashes 80% (drops to 20% of normal) for the rest of the year. To raise your quota, you upgrade the license through 5 paid tiers using **scrip**. The default tier (tier 0) gives you a 12,000 L/year cap; tier 1 raises it to 25,000 L; higher tiers raise further. Per-crop counters reset every March (period 1). The penalty doesn't claw back money you already earned — it just kicks in at the moment you cross the cap, so all subsequent sales of that crop in the same year are at the reduced rate.

### Crop Novelty

The "novelty" system rewards crop rotation. The longer it's been since you sold a particular crop, the higher the price the market pays for it. There are 3 tiers based on the gap (in years) since you last sold that crop: a 1-year gap = no boost (+0%), a 2+ year gap = +15%, and 5+ years (or never sold) = +25% (the max). The boost is locked in at the start of each year (period 1) based on your selling history, and stays locked for the whole year — selling mid-year doesn't drop the bonus until next March's recompute. The boost stacks multiplicatively with the salePrice skill, the licensing penalty, and the boutique skill.

### Boutique

### Mastery & Prestige

The Mastery system tracks two parallel "fame" counters: a per-crop counter (how many liters of WHEAT you've sold ever, how many of BARLEY, etc.) and a global "prestige" counter (every harvestable liter you've ever sold, summed across all crops). Each counter has its own level curve: per-crop levels start at 10,000 L for L1 and grow by 10% per level (so L2 = 11,000 L more, L3 = 12,100 L more, etc.); prestige starts at 24,000 L for L1 with the same growth curve. Per-crop mastery levels grant yield bonuses every 10 levels starting at L5 (+5% yield per tier, so L5 / L15 / L25 / ...) and price bonuses every 10 levels starting at L10 (+5% per tier, so L10 / L20 / L30 / ...). ### Crop Spoilage

Every period change (one in-game month), 3% of all grain stored in your silos and grain trailers spoils — vanishes from the fill level. The intent is to discourage hoarding huge crop stockpiles forever and to reward turnover. Spoilage is scoped to grain only: water, manure, liquid fertilizer, livestock products, and processed goods are exempt. Combines are also exempt (their grain tank is mobile working storage, not warehousing). Auger wagons DO spoil (they're technically trailers per the engine). The Spoilage Immunity skill reduces the rate per level.

### Opposition Events

Once a year (in March, period 1), the cartel rolls a set of "opposition events" against your farm. The number of events that fire is gated by your **pressure score** = `(Cartel shares owned) − (Warchest defense points)`, where defense points = `floor(Warchest balance / $10,000,000)`. So owning lots of shares draws cartel attention; banking lots of cash in the Warchest reduces it. Each effect type rolls independently against the score (higher score = more likely). Effects come in two shapes: **continuous** (apply for the rest of the year — cartel tariff, crop embargo, smear campaign, license revocation, asset registration fee, loan rate hike) and **one-shot** (fire once when activated and never again that year — crop poisoning halves one silo's stash of one crop, cartel audit at P12, goon visit). Effects rotate via a 1-year cooldown — anything that rolled this year can't roll next year. Three debug commands let you control the system: `farmPunkForceOpposition <effectName>`, `farmPunkClearOpposition`, and `farmPunkRerollOpposition`.

### Vehicle Storage Enforcement

Every period change (one in-game month) FarmPunk runs three independent rolls against any owned equipment that's left outside a SHEDS placeable: a guaranteed wear pass that adds +5% damage to every exposed item, a mutually-exclusive "vandal/theft/nothing" 10/5/85 split, and an independent 33% chance of consumable theft (pallets, big bags, IBCs). Vandals damage a small cascade of items starting from the most valuable; theft permanently deletes the most valuable item and detaches anything attached to it; consumable theft removes 1+ random pallets. There is no save file — every period rebuilds the exposure list from scratch by walking the live placeable + vehicle tables. The Insurance Policy and Guard Dogs prestige perks soften theft loss and reduce the roll probabilities respectively. This section tests each of the three rolls in isolation, plus the supporting classifiers (sheltered vs exposed, equipment vs consumable, the damage cutoff).

### Shelter Detection

This is the primitive that decides whether a given vehicle is "inside a shed" or "outside in the rain." The Vehicle Storage Enforcement system (VST-NN above) calls into this every period to figure out which vehicles are exposed and which are protected. The classifier is intentionally simple: a placeable counts as a shed only if its store-item *category* is literally "SHEDS" (so Farm Barn and Old Barn yes, Cow Barn no — even though "barn" is in all three names). The geometry test is a standard 2D rectangle check against the shed's interior area, with corner positions baked into world space at placement time so rotation just works without extra math. The module is stateless — every query rebuilds from the live placeable + vehicle tables, so adding/removing sheds during a session is reflected immediately.

### Black Markets

Black Markets are a parallel selling channel that pay better than the regular selling stations, BUT each market has a yearly volume goal you have to hit and a per-tier eligibility gate (Prestige level + crop Mastery level). When you hit a market's goal liters of the matching crop, the payout fires automatically (cash + scrip + Mastery fame). Markets that you don't complete by year-end just silently lapse. Vendors have crop affinities — e.g. a bakery is more likely to want WHEAT.

### Weather

The weather system rolls one weather per year (every March, on the period-1 tick) and locks it in for the rest of the calendar year. Each weather has three signed knobs: a yield modifier (good for crops or bad for crops), a spoilage modifier (more or less rot in storage), and an exposure modifier (more or less weather damage to gear left outside). The catalog has 45+ weathers spread across five rarities (50% / 25% / 15% / 8% / 2% chance per year). The Forecasting Leveraging perk is an asymmetric amplifier — it boosts the player-favorable side only (good weather gets better; bad weather isn't made worse). ### Console-Command Cleanup Parity

The mod registers ~48 `farmPunk*` console commands across many module files. When the mod unloads (you exit to the main menu), it has to deregister every single one of those commands by name. If a command gets added in one of the modules but its name is forgotten in the central cleanup list, that command "leaks" — it stays registered after the mod unloads, and weird things happen on reload (or on a different save). A previous bug (`f1b3903` in git history) was exactly this: someone added `farmPunkBuyShare` and forgot to add it to the cleanup list. Same kind of bug shipped a second time with `farmPunkShares` (caught and fixed during this audit).

The mod now has a self-check that compares two lists every time you load a save: (1) the names of all `farmPunk*` commands that actually got registered, recorded automatically as each module starts up, and (2) the static `AgriCorp.CONSOLE_COMMANDS` list at the top of `AgriCorp.lua` that the cleanup function iterates. If the two lists don't match, it prints a warning. The two rows below test the OK case and the broken case.

#### Purpose

Verifies in one short pass that AgriCorp loads, persists, reacts to console testing tools, and responds to a single year-long period advance.
